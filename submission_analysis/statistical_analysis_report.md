## 7. Nominal budget, samples, tokens and compute

Four quantities are easily confused and are kept apart in this analysis:

- **Nominal sampling budget k** (`k_max`): the maximum number of samples per problem that the policy may draw (1, 10, 100, 1000). It is the experimental condition and the x-axis of the figures, but not the amount actually spent.
- **Actual samples k_used**: the number of responses actually drawn for a problem before early stopping ended sampling (`k_used` in the CSVs; at least 10 for k ≥ 10). Its mean is reported in section 1; at k = 100 and 1000 it is far below k.
- **API token consumption**: prompt + completion tokens of the used responses, as reported by the API (`token_usage.csv`, below). It depends on k_used and on response length, which differs strongly between models and datasets. Prompt tokens are charged for every sample because each sample is a separate request.
- **Actual computational cost**: the floating-point operations (FLOPs) or energy spent on the provider's hardware. It is **not measured** in this study and cannot be derived from token counts: parameter counts and serving details of gpt-4.1-mini and gpt-4o-mini are not public, so equal token counts of the two models do not imply equal compute. `runtime_seconds` is not a substitute either (see the validation report).

## 8. How sampling was executed

For each individual problem the samples are generated **sequentially**, one request after another in `call_index` order (`for call_idx in range(k_max)` in `eval.py`). After every response the vote is updated and the stopping rules are checked, which is what makes early stopping possible: sample c + 1 is only requested if the first c samples did not satisfy a stopping rule.

Parallelism is across problems, not within a problem: `ThreadPoolExecutor` (64 / 48 / 32 workers for the three runs) runs the sampling loops of different problems concurrently. The worker count affects only wall-clock time; replaying every row with a single worker reproduces the results exactly. In this study, "parallel sampling" denotes the method — independent samples of the same prompt aggregated by majority vote — not concurrent requests for one problem.

**Possible training-data contamination (unresolved).** All selected problems, together with their answers and many worked solutions, were publicly available on the internet before either model was released (gpt-4o-mini in July 2024, gpt-4.1-mini in April 2025). It is therefore possible that some problems or solutions were part of the models' training data, which could inflate absolute accuracy, especially for older, widely discussed problems. This study contains no test for contamination and the providers do not publish their training data, so contamination is neither established nor excluded. Accuracy differences between competition years would not settle the question either, because problem difficulty and style also changed over the years. The within-model comparisons across k are less affected than absolute accuracies, since every k uses the same problems, but a memorised answer could still make agreement among samples, and therefore early stopping, more likely.

- `fig8_gpt41mini_stages.png`: gpt-4.1-mini on the same 300 AIME problems in three separate stages: the original run (2048-token limit, original parser), the post-hoc rescoring of those same responses with the conservative parser (original early-stopping decisions kept), and the final 16384-token run. These are not equivalent runs and must not be pooled. The step from the corrected rescoring to the final run changes the token limit, the early-stopping implementation and the responses themselves at the same time, so the difference cannot be attributed to the token limit alone.
- `fig9_token_usage.png`: Total prompt + completion tokens of the responses that enter the votes at each k (300 problems). Tokens are an API-usage measure, not a FLOP count; tokens of different models are not comparable as compute.
